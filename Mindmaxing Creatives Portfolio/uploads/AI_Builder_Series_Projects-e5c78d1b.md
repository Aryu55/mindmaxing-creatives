## WHAT THIS IS

A receipts series. Not a funnel, not a pitch, no CTA, no link in bio. You show what you made and what making it taught you. If one pops, good. That's the whole strategy.

**These are thought-fuel briefs, not scripts.** Same rule as the transition plan: you freestyle, this doc just makes sure you don't forget what the thing actually does or what the real point was.

## THE ONE RULE THAT MAKES THIS A SERIES INSTEAD OF A PORTFOLIO

Nobody watches "here's an app I built." Everybody watches "here's a problem I couldn't stop thinking about, so I built the thing."

**The project is the receipt. The problem is the video.**

Every episode leads with a problem the viewer recognises — an employer who never paid the full and final, a builder who took token money, a girl who wasn't real. The build is the back half. You're not showing off software. You're showing that you notice things and then you go do something about it.

That framing is also why this works as organic content while being the most persuasive thing you could possibly post. You never mention the course. You never have to.

## WHY THERE ARE NO HOOKS IN THIS DOC

The transition plan's hook doctrine — implied damage, accusation aimed at the viewer — is built for the wound and flip phases. Pointing that at a settlement app makes you sound like a scam ad. For this series the opening line is just the true first sentence of the story, said cold. The story does the work.

Each episode below gives you an OPENER. It's a starting point, not a script. Say your version.

## EP 0 — "I have eight of these and I can't code"

**OPENER:** "I've built eight products in the last year and a half. I still can't write code."

**THE ONE IDEA:** Set the frame for the whole series in thirty seconds. You are not a developer. You noticed things that annoyed you or annoyed people around you, and the cost of building dropped to near zero, so you built them. Some earned money. Some nobody used. You're going to show all of them either way.

**THE HONEST PART:** Say up front that not all of these worked. That single sentence buys you every other episode. A series where everything is a win reads as an ad.

**ON SCREEN:** Fast cut of all eight live URLs loading. That's the cold open. No talking over it.

## EP 1 — Hisaab
`hisaab-lilac-rho.vercel.app`

**WHAT IT IS:** Full and final settlement recovery. You answer about ten questions about your employment, exit date and salary. It calculates what you're actually owed under wage payment law, then generates a five-document recovery pack: three escalating demand emails, a formatted legal notice PDF, a Labour Commissioner complaint draft, gratuity forms, and dispatch instructions. ₹500. Free calculation first. Says clearly it is not a law firm.

**REAL NUMBERS ON THE SITE:** ₹1.84 crore computed in claims. ₹87,400 average claim. Four-day typical turnaround.

**THE ONE IDEA:** Most people who get robbed on their FnF don't fight it because fighting it looks like hiring a lawyer, and hiring a lawyer over ₹80,000 feels stupid. So they eat it. The gap was never the law. The law is already on their side. The gap was the paperwork. That's a document problem, and document problems are exactly what AI dissolved.

**THE HONEST PART:** Did anyone actually pay the ₹500? Is the ₹1.84 crore computed or recovered? Say the real version. If it's computed and not collected, saying that out loud is worth more than the number.

**ON SCREEN:** Run your own numbers into it live. Show the generated legal notice. That document appearing is the whole video.

## EP 2 — Before Token
`before-token.vercel.app`

**WHAT IT IS:** Real estate risk screening for Maharashtra. Checks a project against MahaRERA registrations, tower details and extension history; searches NCLT records for builder insolvency; compares the marketing brochure against the official registry text to catch contradictions; flags missing occupancy certificates. Returns High Risk / Caution / Low Risk.

**THE ONE IDEA:** The brochure-versus-registry check is the interesting part and you should spend the video on it. A builder can print anything on a brochure. The registry says what's actually filed. Nobody cross-reads those two documents because nobody has the time, and that gap is where families lose their entire savings. You made a machine that reads both.

**THE HONEST PART:** This one is Maharashtra-only and it's screening, not due diligence — the site says so. Say it in the video. And if it never got users, that's the episode: "I built a thing that catches builder fraud and I could not get anyone to run one check before paying twenty lakh."

**ON SCREEN:** Run a real project through it. Show a real flag firing.

## EP 3 — SafeSpot
`trysafespot.com`

**WHAT IT IS:** "Know Who He Really Is." Pre-date verification for women. Cross-references public records, court filings and social profiles using phone, photo, email and location so it doesn't false-match. Returns a report separating verified signals from uncertain ones. The subject is never notified. Photos and data are deleted after processing. The report stays on her device.

**THE ONE IDEA:** Keep it safety-framed the entire time, exactly as your transition plan says. The video is about how easy a fake person is to assemble now, which is precisely why checking became necessary. Not surveillance. Verification.

**THE HONEST PART:** The privacy architecture is the flex here. Delete-after-processing and on-device reports are choices you made, and most people building this would have kept the data. Say why you didn't.

**ON SCREEN:** The report generating. Blur everything identifying.

**NOTE:** Your transition plan already assigns SafeSpot videos 25-30. Don't shoot both. This episode replaces #25, and the raw build-vlog and changelog formats from #27 and #29 stay as the ongoing slot after this series ends.

## EP 4 — Pause
MindMaxing's blocker app

**WHAT IT IS:** The Android blocker you built for your own problem.

**THE ONE IDEA:** This is the bridge episode and the most loaded one you have. Nine years, a hundred failed attempts, and at some point you stopped looking for an app that worked and made one. Then — and this is the actual point — you found out the app was never the fix. You'd already written this in your own arc: blockers treat the symptom, the alarm is purposelessness. So the honest version of this episode is "I built the best version of the wrong solution."

**THE HONEST PART:** That's it, above. Don't soften it. It's the only episode where you get to say a product you built didn't solve the problem it was built for, and that admission is worth more than every other episode combined.

**ON SCREEN:** The app running on your actual phone. Nothing polished.

## EP 5 — Glaze
`getglaze.in`

**WHAT IT IS:** "Do you wanna know what your friends really think about you?" Enter your name, get a link, share it, receive anonymous responses. NGL-style.

**THE ONE IDEA:** The one you built for fun, and the one that teaches the most about distribution. It's the simplest product on the list by a mile, and simplicity was never the bottleneck — anonymous-feedback apps live or die entirely on whether the link spreads. Building it took a weekend. Getting it shared was the actual product, and that part has nothing to do with AI.

**THE HONEST PART:** If it didn't spread, that IS the episode. "I built this in a weekend, it worked perfectly, and about eleven people used it." The lesson lands harder than a win would.

**ON SCREEN:** Generate a link live. Send it to the comments if you want — that's not a CTA, that's the demo.

## EP 6 — WhatsApp automation
`whisper-buddy-21.mindmaxing.workers.dev`

**WHAT IT IS:** "Your webinar funnel, on WhatsApp autopilot." Payment triggers an instant WhatsApp confirmation. Abandoned checkouts get chased. A five-stage reminder sequence fires at 2 days, 1 day, 2 hours, 30 minutes and 5 minutes before the webinar. A support bot trained on your docs answers students 24/7 and escalates when it isn't confident. One admin panel.

**THE ONE IDEA:** The unsexy one that actually made money, and the reason why: you didn't invent it, you had the problem. You were running webinars and losing people between payment and showing up. Every other product on this list started with a problem you observed. This one started with a problem that was costing you money that week. That's the difference between an idea and a build.

**THE HONEST PART:** This runs your real business. Show the actual reminder log. Real sends, real numbers, blur the phone numbers.

**ON SCREEN:** The admin panel. A real reminder firing.

## EP 7 — Manifest
`manifest.leblessed.com/lp01`

**WHAT IT IS:** A complete funnel and site you built for a client. ₹197 digital book, down from ₹999. Countdown timer, problem section, sample chapter, testimonials, FAQ, 30-day guarantee, multiple checkout buttons. Headline: "Manifestation Without the Weird Stuff. You built the entire machine — page, offer structure, objection handling, checkout. This is the episode where you talk about the part nobody thinks is a skill: sequencing a page so a stranger keeps reading.

**THE HONEST PART:** Two things to check before you shoot. Get the client's okay to show it. And the testimonials on that page — if they're composite or unverified, don't put them on camera. You've been bitten on fabricated proof before and this series only works if every frame is real.

## EP 8 — Janus
`janus-engine.vercel.app`

**WHAT IT IS:** The one in progress. Content marketing command centre — multi-business content pipeline, Instagram DM automation, analytics, intelligence tooling. Currently behind a login.

**THE ONE IDEA:** End the series on something unfinished. Everything before this was a finished thing shown after the fact, which is the easy version. This one you show mid-build, broken parts included.

**THE HONEST PART:** You spent a day this week debugging why the dashboard threw a server error on every load. The answer was a redirect on the homepage that Next.js baked into a dead 307 during the build, and five earlier fixes had all been aimed at the wrong file. Nobody in the AI-build space posts that.

**ON SCREEN:** The actual error screen. The actual fix. Don't clean it up.

# SHOOTING NOTES

- **~45-60s each, not 30.** These are stories with a demo. The transition plan's 30s cap is for takes; this needs room to show the thing working.
- **Every episode shows the product running.** If you can't show it on screen, it isn't an episode.
- **No CTA. No link in bio. No "DM me."** The series is the proof. Adding a CTA converts it into an ad and kills the only thing it has going for it.
- **Failures stay in.** Two or three of these didn't work. Those are the episodes people finish.
- **Numbers are real or they're cut.** ₹1.84 crore is on the Hisaab site — know whether that's computed or recovered before you say it on camera.
- **Post one a week.** Nine weeks. If one pops, that project gets its own follow-up thread and the series keeps going.
