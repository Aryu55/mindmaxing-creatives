## Working principles to bring into future strategy

1. **Sell one buyer problem with a reason to act now.** Start with the buyer's workflow, costs and goals, then choose the technical solution. A business hiring, expanding, requesting help or experiencing a relevant repeated complaint provides a hypothesis of urgency, not proof of budget or intent. Rank niche candidates by access, ability to pay, problem urgency and delivery fit. Sources: T01, T04, T09, T11, T19, T23.

2. **Proof before price needs qualification.** Preserve Mindmaxing's ability to demonstrate useful work quickly, but show a fix tied to the owner's problem. The roughly 300 unsuccessful mockup attempts in T07 and the unwanted Shopify-audit bundle in T09 limit the legacy brief's overconfident claims about demos closing automatically. Use small verified observations for unknown prospects; reserve substantial bespoke demo time for a warm introduction, explicit interest or unusually strong fit.

3. **Keep outreach brief and make the next step easy.** Two recurring structures are observation → relevant proof → simple question (T04) and observation → problem/consequence → mechanism → soft ask (T19). Their 80–90 word guidance is a heuristic. Use only observations actually checked and proof actually attributable to Mindmaxing; no invented revenue loss, testimonials, urgency or demo completion.

4. **Treat recent negative reviews as a signal, not an identity-resolution system.** T01 reports 100 sends, 15 replies and two deals. It does not establish who was contacted or how a public reviewer became a verified business contact. Distinguish a business with service failures from a buyer dissatisfied with a competing provider. Use permitted sources, verify the business relationship, and never infer a private identity from a pseudonymous complaint. Human review and signal freshness are part of the process.

5. **Measure stage loss before increasing volume.** Track unique prospects, attempts, accepted deliveries, human replies, interested replies, qualified opportunities, booked and held meetings, proposals, paid wins, margin and time separately. T06 has replies and calls but budget/timing problems; T16 has many DMs and changing offers but no usable conversion denominator. Check delivery, fit, proof, authority and offer before prescribing more messages or another channel.

6. **Borrow real trust and build repeatable introductions.** Complementary designers, marketers, IT providers, accountants and business connectors can improve access. Record who referred whom and ask specifically after useful work, rather than hoping for referrals. T03, T05, T15, T20 and T23 support the mechanism but not guaranteed yield. Recurring posts from the same authors are not independent evidence.

7. **Use bounded pilots and price ongoing work separately.** A paid pilot is the default candidate to test commitment and reduce scope risk; free micro-proofs may be useful, but no open-ended free delivery. T14 reports success with free management while clients funded ad spend; T19 and T21 warn about zero-price anchoring and unsuitable clients. Retainers require genuine recurring value, support capacity and their own economics. Never promise sales, views or leads outside our control.

8. **Protect distribution, evidence and capacity while delivering.** T08's $45k MRR/16-hour days and T15/T17/T22's concentration stories are warnings. Keep per-client operating notes, a bounded acquisition routine, agreed communication windows, permissioned proof capture and visibility into client/introducer concentration. Solve solo capacity with scope, systems and workload limits before assuming hires are authorized.

9. **The 1,000-Send Discipline (Document First, Change After).** Never thrash campaigns, rewrite copy, or pivot positioning based on small sample sizes (n=25 or n=50). Two auto-replies or one bounce is statistical noise, not a structural trend. Commit to running the full 1,000-prospect cohort. Log every incoming signal, AI deflection bot, ticket-system edge case, and human reply rigorously in the cohort ledger. Macro changes to targeting, channel mix, offer structure, or copy are evaluated and implemented only after completing the 1,000-send milestone.

10. **The Mechanical Pipe Invariant (Repair Routes, Never Thrash Offers).** Distinguish between *Proposition Thrashing* (changing what you sell or slashing prices because of impatience) and *Mechanical Routing Repairs* (stopping dispatches to customer service helpdesks and AI deflection bots). If 88% of scraped leads are generic `support@` or `info@` addresses, continuing to send developer pitches to ticketing queues only measures Zendesk's filter efficiency, not market demand. Hardwire scrapers and dispatchers to **fail-closed** on verified founders only. Fixing the recipient route protects the statistical integrity of the 1,000-send test.

11. **Automated Delivery Monitoring and Empirical Volume Progression over Synthetic Warmup.** Synthetic P2P warmup between mailboxes on the same host (IONOS) does not establish domain reputation at Google Workspace or Microsoft 365. Google Postmaster Tools and spam filters evaluate traffic entering their own MX infrastructure, not internal server loops. Replace unverified synthetic exchanges with empirical diagnostic monitoring: send 1 diagnostic message/mailbox/day to real personal Gmail test inboxes with strict read-only IMAP inspection (`BODY.PEEK[]`, never flagging read or replying). Enforce a strict Level 1 volume cap (1 campaign message/mailbox/day) for 7 consecutive days, auto-pausing immediately on Spam placement or SPF/DKIM failure, and promote volume only when empirical authentication and inbox placement pass.

12. **The Technical Incident Qualification Rule (Incident Evidence over Inferred Budget).** The engineering sprint offer ($500–$1,000 fixed-scope) requires an actionable storefront technical incident, not general marketing advice. The previous failure was mistaking ordinary store feedback ("traffic but no sales", "people add to cart but don't buy", "review my store", "ads not working") for an engineering breakdown. Qualification means "worth investigating technical malfunction," not an inferred budget, inferred margin, or guessing whether an operator is "poor" based on a Gmail address or daily ad spend. An outreach signal qualifies only when an author reports a specific, current malfunction on a relevant storefront component (cart drawer, quantity selector, add-to-cart, checkout button, liquid glitch) connected in the same or adjacent sentence within seven days, with verified operator ownership. Record explicitly stated commercial facts separately from conclusions.

- T06: 448 outreaches, 33 responses, 21 Looms, five calls are supplied by a commenter. That is 7.37% replies and 1.12% calls/outreach; closed-sales count is unavailable.

- T20: 1%+ total and 0.5%+ interested replies are commenter benchmarks, not measured Mindmaxing thresholds.
- T04's €8k weeks and T09's 600+ historical clients are unverified experience claims. T18's nearly €11k is order volume, not MRR or collected cash.
- T13's paid funnel is hypothetical. T21's CAC is ad-only and geography/offer-specific. Do not compare it directly to fully loaded acquisition cost.

Use the ledger before quoting any numerical claim. Do not average incompatible denominators, channels, periods, countries or currencies.
