# Applying the dossier to Mindmaxing

These are proposed adaptations, not historical results or a launched campaign. Read [strategic memory](../strategic-memory.md), [the thread index](thread-index.md) and [the evidence ledger](benchmarks.md) with this document. Thread IDs below resolve in the index. Bracketed text in copy is an input to fill truthfully, not a claim to send as written.

## 1. Signal-led outreach pipeline

Suggested stages: **observed signal → source/identity check → fit and capacity check → human-reviewed draft → authorized outreach → reply triage → discovery/proof → paid work → results review → appropriate repeat work/introduction**.

Keep one record per business/contact with: source URL; captured date; event date if available; signal type; exact observation; reliability; affected workflow; candidate decision-maker and how their business role is known; contact-data source; permitted channel; existing customer/provider relationship if known; offer hypothesis; evidence available; estimated prep effort; owner; next step/date; suppression status; and outcome. Store only the information needed for the business purpose. Capture dates are not necessarily event dates.

Qualify on four questions from T09/T11/T19: Can we solve this problem? Does this buyer care now? Can they pay and decide? Can we deliver it sustainably? Use unknown rather than inventing answers. A poor-looking website, open role, negative review or firmographic match is not sufficient alone.

| Signal | Potential Mindmaxing fit | Check before drafting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explicit business request for a website, integration or operational fix | One bounded implementation | Real buyer, scope, recency, budget and allowed response channel |
| Recent repeated complaints about business response delays | Enquiry routing, booking or WhatsApp handling | Complaints describe a fixable process; routing/staff coverage are the actual bottleneck |
| Manufacturer launching products or opening distribution | Product catalogue or enquiry workflow | Genuine expansion, catalogue ownership, existing sales process and decision-maker |
| A measured broken enquiry/checkout path | Targeted conversion or technical repair | Reproduce the defect; avoid inventing lost revenue or assuming access to analytics |
| Hiring for a relevant operations role | A workflow-assistance hypothesis | Hiring can indicate spending, but the role may need human judgement rather than replacement |
| Trusted partner introduction | Small problem-led demo and scoped proposal | Intro permission, actual need, commercial owner and capacity |

T01 supports a transferable timing hypothesis, not a mandatory Trustpilot scraper. Use accessible sources through allowed methods. Refresh a signal before outreach if the review queue has delayed it. Define queue capacity from actual human review time; reduce intake when drafts age rather than automating sends to clear the backlog.

## 2. The two review-signal routes must stay distinct

**Route A — improve the reviewed business.** A business's own public customers report a recurring process problem. If Mindmaxing has a relevant fix, reach its appropriate business contact through a suitable channel. Refer to a verified category of friction sensitively; do not amplify allegations or assume every review is accurate.

**Route B — help a buyer dissatisfied with an alternative provider.** A buyer explicitly describes a problem with a competing service. It is usable only when the buyer's business identity and suitable contact route are actually known and the need fits Mindmaxing. Do not deanonymize a reviewer, infer employment from a display name, or pitch unrelated services just because a review is negative.

The original T01 mixes these routes. It does not supply a reproducible identity/contact-enrichment method. Record unresolved identity and skip dependent outreach rather than treating the missing step as solved.

## 3. Proof before price with an effort gate

For unknown prospects, start with a small verified observation or a permission-based offer of a short example. For qualified replies or trusted introductions, prepare a narrow prototype tied to the discussed workflow. The legacy 45–90 minute build is a historical prep estimate, not the cost to spend on every cold account.

Examples aligned with existing technical services:

- A manufacturer lacks a usable catalogue enquiry path: demonstrate one realistic product-to-enquiry flow, not a full speculative rebrand.
- A business misses enquiries after hours: show intake and routing with clearly labelled demo data, then verify who can follow up.
- A D2C buyer has a verified checkout defect: demonstrate that defect and a proposed correction; project revenue impact only with an explicit model and client-supplied inputs.

Never portray demo transactions, simulated traffic or an unfinished prototype as deployed customer results. T07's failed unsolicited mockups and T09's unused ten-store audit packs show that effort alone does not establish demand.

## 4. Copy frameworks to adapt

### First contact: observation → commercial relevance → mechanism → permission

> Hi [name] — I noticed [specific, verified business observation]. It looks like [carefully framed workflow problem]. We build [plain-language fix for that problem]. Would a short example of how it could work for [business] be useful?

Adapted from T04/T19. Add one relevant proof point only when there is permissioned, verifiable evidence; otherwise omit it. Keep under roughly 80–90 words as a practical editing target, not a proven magic threshold. A hard calendar ask or unsolicited large attachment is not required on first contact.

### Review-informed contact to a business

> Hi [name] — I noticed recent feedback about [verified process category]. If improving that is on your list, we could help with [specific feasible mechanism]. Would a short outline be useful?

Adapted from T01. Use only when the observation was checked and is proportionate to mention. Do not paste an identifiable complaint, claim private knowledge, or state that a few reviews prove systematic revenue loss.

### Value-adding follow-up

> One thought on [problem]: [new useful observation or small practical suggestion]. Is this relevant to your current plans, or should I leave it here?

T04 favors two total touches; T19 proposes day 0/3/7/14. Choose a bounded initial test and measure useful replies, complaints and opt-outs by touch. Do not infer that either schedule is established best practice for Mindmaxing. A later new-angle campaign is not a loophole around suppression.

### Partner introduction request

> We help [specific buyer] fix [specific problem] through [bounded deliverable]. If that comes up with one of your clients, would you be comfortable introducing us? I can send a short example of the work and the scope first.

Adapted from T03/T05/T09/T20/T21. Agree referral economics against collected revenue, effort and margin before making an offer; neither a Reddit 35% fee nor the legacy 10–20% range is an automatic rule.

### Referral request after delivery

> Now that [verified milestone] is working, do you know one or two [fitting businesses] dealing with [same problem]? An introduction would help; I can send a short note you can forward.

Adapted from T19 and the referral cases. Do not use invented scarcity such as “only two slots” unless it is true.

## 5. From interested reply to paid pilot

1. **Confirm the problem in the buyer's words.** Ask what happens now, how often, who handles it and why a change matters. T11 shows an apparently inefficient workflow can preserve something the buyer values.
2. **Check the buying conditions.** Authority, timing, workable budget, stakeholder access and delivery dependencies are separate from interest. T06's budget-blocked calls are not a reason to rewrite every subject line.
3. **Show the smallest relevant proof.** Use a short walkthrough of the verified problem and proposed mechanism. Separate measured facts, hypotheses and demo behaviour.
4. **Offer a written bounded engagement.** Define deliverables, acceptance criteria, deadline/dependencies, fee, payment milestones, included revisions, exclusions and the client's work. A pilot can stand alone; do not disguise indefinite free labour as qualification.
5. **Record the decision and next step.** Label objections as timing, budget, authority, trust, priority, fit or delivery concern. A nurture date is appropriate when the buyer requests later contact; persistent pressure is not qualification.
6. **Review results and scope ongoing value.** Ongoing maintenance or optimization should have its own deliverables and price. Capture permissioned before/after evidence and ask for a suitable introduction after delivery.

This adapts T03/T06/T09/T14/T19/T21/T23. Prefer controllable commitments over commercial outcome guarantees. No proposed fee or specific engagement term is approved solely by this dossier.

## 6. Funnel diagnosis and metrics

Use a cohort window and deduplicate prospects. Track message count separately from unique people. A suggested reporting convention is total human replies / unique recipients with at least one accepted message; accepted messages are not proof of inbox placement. Keep a second, explicitly named per-send rate only if needed for comparison. Exclude bounces and automated replies from human replies; label negative replies and opt-outs separately.

| Symptom | Check first | Candidate intervention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No replies | Delivery evidence, invalid addresses, source quality, recipient role, message relevance | Repair the affected upstream step before increasing volume |
| Replies but little interest | Actual need, timing, proof, whether replies are negative or automated | Sharpen the specific problem and qualification |
| Interest but no meeting/progress | Ask friction, stakeholder access, next-step clarity | Offer a smaller useful next step and clarify who decides |
| Calls but no paid work | Budget, urgency, risk, scope, credibility, sales process | Improve discovery and bounded offer; record reasons |
| Pilots but no repeat work | Whether recurring value exists, result evidence, full-price economics | Fix delivery/fit or keep a one-off offer |
| Growth with overload | Founder hours, support load, margins and active commitments | Tighten scope, automate repeatable work and cap intake |

Minimum useful records: source/campaign, unique business/person ID, first contact date, touch count, delivery state, human reply classification, qualified status, meeting booked/held, proposal, paid win, cash received, delivery costs/hours, support hours, referral source and next step. Fully loaded acquisition cost includes attributed acquisition labor and tools, not just ads. Do not count bookings, order value, recurring revenue and profit interchangeably.

Use the ledger's small cohorts to form hypotheses, not targets. Document a test's buyer segment, channel, observation type, offer, message variant, follow-up bounds, observation window and effort cap. Change one major variable at a time when practical. Review a campaign early for clear problems; a suggested 60–90 day focus does not mean continuing through failures blindly.

## 7. Operational memory and maintenance

For each active client, keep one concise operating record: scope, ICP, assumptions, baselines, what is working, current issues, access owner, communication expectations, next milestone and next action. Protect time for acquisition even while delivering; track largest-client and largest-introducer share separately. Seek permission to use completed work rather than assuming white-label work can be displayed publicly.

When Mindmaxing accumulates results, append a dated local experiment record and link it here. Preserve the Reddit evidence as historical source material; let current, properly measured Mindmaxing results govern forecast assumptions.
